# Demo 27 physics roadmap

What each sub-demo is for, what evidence put it where it is, and why the running
order is what it is.

## Source documents

Two working notes in `docs/equation_2_from_paper/` are the origin of this
campaign and should be read alongside it:

- `Nextnano_Professional_Physics_Roadmap.md` — the nine physics categories (A-I),
  their tiers and their per-category priority. Demo 27's sub-demos are that
  document's categories, renumbered: A → 27A/27B/27C, C → 27C/27K, D → 27D,
  E → 27E, F → 27G/27H/27L, G → 27J, H → 27M, I → 27N.
- `Nextnano_Pro_Physics_Capture_Reference.md` — what is worth saving while a
  Professional run is already happening. It is what the `harvest:` block in
  `demo27_config.yaml` implements.

Two scope notes where Demo 27 deliberately does less than those documents list:

- **Category D's Tier-3 sensitivity axes** — central barrier thickness, well
  width, Al composition, grading width, interface profile shape, interface
  roughness — are **not** in 27D. 27D answers one question, abrupt versus 1 nm
  graded, and adding sensitivity axes to it would turn a controlled comparison
  into a sweep. If 27D shows structure matters, a sensitivity sub-demo is the
  right follow-up and gets its own cost statement.
- **Category H's strain outputs** are in the harvest as "if computed" only. This
  is a lattice-matched GaAs/AlₓGa₁₋ₓAs system on a GaAs substrate, so no deck in
  the campaign computes strain, and requesting it would produce zeros rather than
  physics.

## What is actually still wrong

Demo 26 compared the real Equation 2 spectrum against the digitized Ramesh
Fig. 2d curve and ranked fifteen hypotheses. Its own summary
(`EXTENDED_FEATURE_DIAGNOSIS.csv`) is the target Demo 27 is aiming at:

| Feature | Paper (nm) | Model (nm) | Still unexplained? |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| P1 | 540 | 688 | YES |
| Z1 | 605 | 720 | YES |
| P2 | 760 | 752 | no / near |
| P3 | 1080 | — | no / near |
| Z2 | 1330 | 1455 | YES |
| P4 | 1520 | 1503 | no / near |

Two prior results constrain everything below and must not be re-derived:

- **Demo 24's reachability finding.** The paper's curve is generated by exactly
  two transition energies, whose one- and two-photon features come in 2:1
  wavelength pairs: 760/1520 nm from E = 1.631 eV, and 540/1080 nm from
  E = 2.296 eV. Demo 23D has the first (ΔE₂₂ = 1.648 eV) and nothing near the
  second. 2.296 eV is **unreachable** by any bound-subband pair in that
  structure: the Al₀.₅₅Ga₀.₄₅As barrier gap is 2.145 eV, and where the electron
  subbands cross that edge the largest Eₑ − E_hh has only reached ~1.93–1.97 eV.
  No setting of kmax, subband energy, pathway amplitude, sign, broadening,
  k-grid or normalization creates P1/Z1/P3.
- **Demo 24's k-truncation finding.** The model's own 1250–1450 nm structure is
  an artifact: that trough marched 1502 → 1431 → 1376 → 1318 nm as kmax went
  0.05 → 0.125 π/a and tracked 2hc/ΔE(kmax) to 9 nm, while the two real peaks
  moved less than 1 nm. It must not be compared with the paper's 1330 nm node.

So the campaign is looking for something that changes **which transition
energies exist**, or **how far in k they are integrated**, not for a better
fitting parameter.

| 1 | geometry mismatch (paper abrupt vs our 1 nm grading) | medium-high | yes | **27D** |
| 2 | kmax / BZ convention (0.1 Γ-X could mean 0.2 π/a) | medium-high | yes | **27G** |

are what the Professional runs are for. Ranks 3 and 7 are partly answered by 27B
and 27C and partly need data this project does not have.

## Running order, and where it departs from the plan

The order Demo 27 recommends is **27A → 27D → 27G → 27B → 27E → 27F**, then
evidence decides.

That is not the order in the original brief, which had 27B (finite-k matrix
elements) third and 27G (kmax/BZ) fifth. The brief also said not to trust its
own ordering if the repository audit showed something stronger. It does:

- **Demo 26 ranks the BZ convention 2 of 15 and finite-k M(k) 5 of 15**, and
  gives the BZ hypothesis "YES" against both of the unexplained feature groups.
- **27G is cheaper than 27B** — 1–3 h against 1–3 h plus 10–15 GB, and it needs
  no gate to pass first.
- **27G is not blocked by anything.** 27B cannot start until 27A passes.

27A still goes first, for a reason that has nothing to do with its rank. It is
20 minutes, and it establishes which output keywords make nextnano++ write state
data at finite k. That answer applies to **every later Professional deck in the
campaign**. Running a 3-hour 27G first and discovering afterwards that a better
harvest was available would waste far more than 20 minutes.

27D goes second because it is rank 1, is not blocked, costs 9–30 minutes with the
graded arm reused, and changes exactly one thing.

## The sub-demos

### Tier 0-1: cheap, and they unblock other things

**27A — finite-k output pilot.** *Can Professional export usable finite-k
wavefunctions, spinors and matrix elements at k ≠ 0?* Demo 23 asked for
`all_k_points = yes` and still got only `k00000`, because `all_k_points` follows
the **k-integration** grid and Demo 23 had `k_integration_disabled{}`, leaving one
point. Demo 25's six pilot variants isolate one candidate explanation each. PASS
means O_nm(k), z_e,nl(k) and z_hh,ml(k) can be constructed. FAIL stops 27B.

**27I — solver/model cross-validation.** *Which features come specifically from
the 8-band k.p treatment?* kp6 and single-band arms against the reused kp8 arm.
Cheap, and it tells you whether the disagreement is even a multiband question.

### Tier 2: the main reproduction work

**27D — exact paper geometry.** *Does the paper's abrupt structure reproduce the
spectrum better than our 1 nm graded structure?* One deck, one change. The
geometry is **not** optimized: 27D asks whether structure mismatch explains a
major portion of the disagreement, not which structure fits best.

**27G — kmax and BZ convention.** *What does "one tenth of the Brillouin zone"
physically correspond to?* This repository uses k_BZ = π/a, so production kmax is
0.1 π/a = 0.5557 nm⁻¹. For zincblende along [010], Γ–X is 2π/a, so "0.1 of Γ–X"
— and equally "0.1 of a reciprocal lattice vector" — is **0.2 π/a, twice the
cutoff every result so far has used**. The paper does not say which it means.
Every feature is classified STABLE or CUTOFF ARTIFACT out to 0.3 π/a.

**27C — extended states.** *Are e1, e2, hh1, hh2 enough?* 48 states on the frozen
structure, each carrying a physical identity rather than an energy index — Demo
24 found Demo 23's "hh2" was 97.6% light-hole, precisely because an ordering rule
cannot tell HH from LH. The ~2.296 eV search has a rule built in: an energy match
is never enough, and any candidate above the barrier gap must be flagged rather
than adopted.

**27F — numerical domain convergence.** *Could numerical choices be creating the
higher states?* Mesh, padding, outer barrier, boundary condition and state count,
one per deck. A physical state must not depend strongly on an arbitrary outer
boundary; anything that does is labelled a Dirichlet box state.

**27H — crystal-direction anisotropy.** *Is the radial assumption valid?* Three
intermediate in-plane angles against the [010] and [011] arms Demo 23 already
has. If the spread is negligible, the right outcome is to mark **27L NOT NEEDED**
— that is a successful result that saves a day of solver time.

**27J — temperature and linewidth.** *Do temperature-dependent parameters or
better linewidths explain the remaining shifts?* Two temperature decks, plus a
linewidth study that is post-processing only. Demo 26 already found no single Γ
repairs all features (high confidence), so **Γ is not available as a fitting
parameter here** and a fitted Γ is not an acceptable conclusion.

### Tier 3-4: only if the evidence points there

**27E — Schrödinger vs Schrödinger-Poisson.** Both arms undoped: the paper's
carrier input is unknown (Demo 26's note on hypothesis 4), and Demo 27 will not
invent doping to manufacture a difference. `decks.Doping` refuses a region with
no justification string. A `quantum_poisson` run that exits DONE without
converging is a FAIL, not a result.

**27K — continuum and quasi-bound states.** Gated on 27C *and* 27F, and gated on
evidence: if 27F already labels the high states as box states, the right outcome
is NOT NEEDED. Three domains, because a continuum claim is meaningless unless the
domain is varied underneath it.

**27L — full 2D (kx, ky).** The most expensive calculation in the campaign, and
the one most likely to be unnecessary. Runs only if 27H shows anisotropy above
its tolerance.

**27M — advanced multiband optics.** Spin- and branch-resolved decomposition of
the Equation 2 pathways from 27B's harvest. No decks, no solver — this is the
collect-broadly rule paying for itself.

### Tier 5 and feasibility

**27N — exciton / many-body feasibility.** A feasibility study first, not an
implementation: estimate the binding energy, the Coulomb enhancement and the
screening, and compare them against the size of the gaps in the table at the top
of this document. Building a many-body solver is explicitly out of scope until
that arithmetic says it is warranted. Note that nextnano++ has a native
`transition_energies{ excitons{ electron_mass hole_mass dielectric_const } }`
block, so the first estimate may need no new tooling at all.

**27O — microscopic Bloch matrix element.** *Is r_e,hh(k) ≈ 0.751 nm constant
adequate?* Last on purpose: a microscopic correction to the Bloch factor is only
meaningful once the envelope-function physics above it is understood. The kp8
route uses 27B's harvest and needs no new software; a DFT/HSE06 validation is a
separate provisioning decision this project has not made.

## What would count as success

The campaign succeeds when each of P1, Z1, Z2 is either **explained** — a named
physical cause with a measured effect big enough to close the gap — or
**demonstrated unreachable**, as Demo 24 did for the 2.296 eV transition in the
Demo 23 structure. Retiring a hypothesis is as much a result as confirming one,
and several sub-demos are designed so that a null answer is a PASS.
