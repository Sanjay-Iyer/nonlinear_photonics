## Pass condition

The chi2 spectrum is computed out to at least 0.2 pi/a, and every spectral feature is classified as either STABLE (its position stops moving as kmax grows) or CUTOFF ARTIFACT (its position tracks the zone-edge transition energy). Demo 24 already showed that the model's own 1250-1450 nm structure is the second kind: it marched 1502 -> 1431 -> 1376 -> 1318 nm as kmax went 0.05 -> 0.125 pi/a and tracked 2hc/DeltaE(kmax) to 9 nm, while the two real peaks moved less than 1 nm. PASS means every feature carries that label out to the Gamma-X reading of the paper's cutoff.

## The controlled change

The in-plane cutoff only. The k-point count moves with it so that the k spacing stays at the Demo 23 value - widening the range while holding the point count fixed would confound a cutoff effect with a sampling effect, and Demo 24 has already shown this spectrum is sensitive to both.

Fields this sub-demo may change: `relative_size`, `kmax_per_nm`, `k_points`.
At most **3** per deck. Everything else is inherited verbatim from the Demo 23 baseline, and `framework/registry.resolve` raises rather than generating a deck that breaks either rule.

## The ambiguity, precisely

This repository defines `k_BZ = pi/a`, so production kmax is
`0.1 * pi/a = 0.5557 nm^-1`. The paper says the response saturates at "one-tenth
of the Brillouin zone away from the zone center" without giving its
reciprocal-space convention.

For zincblende along a conventional [010] Gamma-X line, **Gamma-X is 2pi/a**, so
"0.1 of Gamma-X" is `0.2 pi/a` — twice the cutoff every result in this project so
far has used. "0.1 of a reciprocal lattice vector" is likewise `0.2 pi/a`.

Demo 26 ranks this **2 of 15**, medium-high confidence, and marks it YES against
both unexplained feature groups. Demo 24 found appreciable change already at
0.125 pi/a, so this is a live possibility rather than a formality.

## Why the point count moves with the cutoff

The k spacing is held at the Demo 23 value: 301, 451, 601 and 901 points for
0.10, 0.15, 0.20 and 0.30 pi/a. Widening the range while holding the count fixed
would confound a cutoff effect with a sampling effect, and Demo 24 showed this
spectrum is sensitive to both.

## The deliverable

Every feature classified **STABLE** (its position stops moving as kmax grows) or
**CUTOFF ARTIFACT** (its position tracks the zone-edge transition energy).

Demo 24 already established that the model's own 1250-1450 nm structure is the
second kind: it marched 1502 -> 1431 -> 1376 -> 1318 nm as kmax went 0.05 ->
0.125 pi/a and tracked `2hc/DeltaE(kmax)` to 9 nm, while the two real peaks (752,
1503 nm) moved less than 1 nm. **Do not compare that trough with the paper's
1330 nm node.** 27G extends that classification out to the Gamma-X reading.

## A caveat worth stating

Demo 24 also found that extending kmax alone is useless without finite-k `M(k)`:
a frozen numerator never damps the high-k tail, so the artifact relocates rather
than disappearing. 27G can therefore *classify* features honestly, but a
*corrected* high-k tail needs 27B. That is why both are in the campaign.

## Data harvest

Whenever this sub-demo's decks are solved, the shared broad harvest in `demo27_config.yaml` is requested — eigenenergies, envelopes, kp8 spinors, CB/HH/LH/SO character, probabilities, transition energies, dipole and momentum matrix elements, oscillator strengths, band edges, alloy composition, k vectors, dispersions and masses, plus electrostatic potential and strain where the deck already computes them.

No expensive new physics is enabled merely because an output would be interesting. The harvest costs disk, not solver time.
