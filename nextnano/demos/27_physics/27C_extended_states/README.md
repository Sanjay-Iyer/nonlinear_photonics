## Pass condition

E_n(k) is available for every requested state over the whole k path, each state carries a physical identity (CB/HH/LH/SO fractions, localization, confinement) rather than an energy index, and every candidate transition in the search window is classified bound / quasi-bound / continuum. The ~2.296 eV question is answered with evidence, not assumed: Demo 24 showed no bound-bound transition in this structure can exceed the 2.145 eV barrier gap, so a bound candidate there would contradict that and needs the stronger evidence.

## The controlled change

The number of states solved and written. Geometry, mesh, temperature, k range and k sampling are all the Demo 23 baseline, so any new state is a property of the structure and not of a changed calculation.

Fields this sub-demo may change: `num_electrons`, `num_holes`, `output_state_count`.
At most **3** per deck. Everything else is inherited verbatim from the Demo 23 baseline, and `framework/registry.resolve` raises rather than generating a deck that breaks either rule.

## Cost

**Basis:** Demo 23 solved 301 k points for 14 states in 10 min 20 s. The eigenvalue cost grows faster than linearly in the state count, so 48 states is taken as 3-10x that. Envelopes are written at k=0 only unless 27A unlocks per-k output, which is why 27C is cheap compared with 27B.

`outputs/COST_STATEMENT.md` is regenerated by preflight from the resolved configuration, so it reflects the decks that actually exist rather than this document.

## The question, stated carefully

Not "are there more states" — there are always more states in a finite box. The
question is whether any state outside {e1, e2, hh1, hh2} carries enough optical
weight to matter for Equation 2, and whether it is a property of the structure or
of the numerical domain.

## The 2.296 eV search has a rule built into it

Demo 24 showed the paper's 540/1080 nm feature pair implies a transition at
E = 2.296 eV, and that **no bound-bound transition in this structure can exceed
the 2.145 eV barrier gap** (from `bandedges.dat`: CB 3.3244, VB 1.1795 eV). The
electron subbands cross that CB edge at k ~ 0.73-0.85 /nm, where the largest
Ee - Ehh has only reached ~1.93-1.97 eV.

So: **an energy match is never enough.** Every candidate is also scored on
envelope overlap and oscillator strength, and any candidate involving a state
outside the barrier gap is flagged as a Dirichlet box state of the quantum
region, whose energy depends on the region width rather than on the physics. A
*bound* candidate at 2.296 eV would contradict Demo 24 and needs stronger
evidence, not weaker.

This sub-demo also does **not** assume the ~2.296 eV interpretation is correct.
Demo 26's `P1_P3_2296EV_REASSESSMENT.md` records that the paper does not itself
assign both peaks to one transition. 27C reports what states exist and what they
can produce; the interpretation is a separate judgement.

## Identity, not index

Every state is reported with its CB/HH/LH/SO fractions, its localization (which
well, or neither), its confinement relative to the barrier edge, its oscillator
strengths, its transition strengths, and a bound / quasi-bound / continuum label.
An energy-ordered index is not an identity — that is exactly the error Demo 24
found in Demo 23's `hh2`.

## Why it is cheap

E_n(k) for all 48 states over the whole path comes from `dispersion{ path{} }`,
which already works. Full per-k envelopes are what 27A unlocks and 27B pays for.

## Data harvest

Whenever this sub-demo's decks are solved, the shared broad harvest in `demo27_config.yaml` is requested — eigenenergies, envelopes, kp8 spinors, CB/HH/LH/SO character, probabilities, transition energies, dipole and momentum matrix elements, oscillator strengths, band edges, alloy composition, k vectors, dispersions and masses, plus electrostatic potential and strain where the deck already computes them.

No expensive new physics is enabled merely because an output would be interesting. The harvest costs disk, not solver time.
